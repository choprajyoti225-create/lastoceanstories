The Last Light

For more than two centuries, lighthouse keepers lived at the edge of the map, climbing spiral stairs each evening to trim wicks, polish brass, and wind the clockwork that turned the great lenses. Their days were measured in oil, weather, and the slow sweep of the beam across black water; their nights were spent awake so that strangers they would never meet could find their way home. Today almost every light on Earth is automated, but the towers still stand on their lonely headlands, and each flash that crosses the waves carries a quiet echo of the people who once kept it burning.
